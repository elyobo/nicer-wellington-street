Yarra Council votes on Wellington Street on Tuesday 12 May 2026. The nine councillors can still vote for the shared street option, even though council staff are not recommending it. The single most useful thing you can do is register to speak at the meeting.

In short:

  • What’s recommended. A smaller package: 30 km/h speed limit across the area, lighting upgrades, footpath treatments on side streets, repainting the existing bike lanes. Nothing that reduces the volume of cars cutting through Wellington Street.
  • Why it falls short. Staff themselves describe their recommendation as having safety benefits “lower than those of the change options”1 and as “unlikely to receive funding from the State Government as an upgrade to a cycling route.”2 Council’s own consultation found majority support for a change option (64% in Clifton Hill, 62% in Collingwood),3 but staff have recommended neither. The road safety auditors’ two highest-severity recommendations are not delivered either. The staff report also contradicts the two claims that have driven the most opposition: that the proposal closes Wellington Street, and that it will flood side streets with traffic.
  • What you can do this week. Register to speak at the 12 May meeting. If you can’t make Tuesday, call or email three of the councillors today.

Register to speak

Councillors have read the emails. Hearing from you in person, in the room, carries more weight.

Register to speak at the council meeting. Tuesday 12 May, Richmond Town Hall, 333 Bridge Road.
Agenda item 7.1 Building a Safer Wellington Street. Registrations close 6:30pm Monday 11 May.

Register to speak →

You don’t need to be a polished public speaker. Each speaker has up to two minutes, but you don’t have to use the full time. Saying you support Option 1 (the shared street), a sentence on why, and sitting down is enough. If you’ve used Wellington Street on foot, by bike, or in a car, that’s the experience to bring.

Two helpful points to make: the consultation, on staff’s own numbers, shows local support for change at least matches local opposition in both sections; and much of the opposition rests on claims the staff report itself contradicts.

What council staff are recommending

The proposed package on Wellington Street and its surrounding streets:

  • A 30 km/h speed limit across the area bounded by Alexandra Parade, Smith Street, Queens Parade and Hoddle Street.
  • Repainting the existing painted bike lanes (no change to their layout). The bike-lane line marking and green paint were refreshed earlier in 2026; this is routine maintenance.4
  • Lighting upgrades on Hodgkinson Street near the church and the roundabout.
  • In Clifton Hill (the North Section): new continuous footpath treatments on Council Street and Noone Street.
  • In Collingwood (the South Section): upgrades to the existing continuous footpaths on Hotham Street and Easey Street, defined in the agenda as smoothing the bluestone on the roadway and re-line-marking.5
  • Raised pedestrian crossings at three Gold Street intersections (Hotham, Keele, Sackville). These were originally proposed by Stantec as part of the mitigation package for any traffic displaced from Wellington Street if traffic barriers were installed there. It’s unclear why these are being prioritised over treatments that would make it safer and easier for locals to cross Wellington Street, which will retain far higher traffic volumes under the recommended package.6
  • Sightline and vegetation work near intersections, including some car park removals.
  • A small widening of the bike lane at the south side of the Wellington and Alexandra Parade intersection.

What it does not include: any traffic calming on Wellington Street itself, any reduction in the volume of cars cutting through, or physically separated bike lanes. A full-time zebra crossing at Clifton Hill Primary is mentioned as one possible upgrade among several but is not committed to.

The recommendation is to investigate and design these works, not to commit to building them. The $200,000 in next year’s draft budget is for design and feasibility. Whether anything is actually built depends on grants, and staff themselves rate the strongest grant pathway (state cycling-corridor funding) as unlikely.2

Why this falls short

It doesn’t address the main problem. Wellington Street is heavily used as a non-local through-route. In Collingwood, council’s own monitoring found that up to 88% of southbound morning traffic at Alexandra Parade had no local origin or destination.7 The recommendation explicitly “rule[s] out anything that impacts traffic volumes,” in the staff report’s own words.8 What’s recommended leaves that volume in place and adds maintenance and lighting around it.

The road safety auditors’ highest-severity recommendations are not fully delivered. Council’s road safety audit made eleven findings. Two were rated highest-severity for fatal and serious injury and carried “Primary” recommendations (defined by the auditors as those that “practically eliminate” the risk). One covers the dooring risk along the entire length of Wellington Street, where the auditors recommend kerb-protected bike lanes between kerb and parking; the recommendation does not deliver this. The other is the Hodgkinson Street roundabout, where the auditors recommend speed cushions on approach to bring speeds down to the Safe System tolerance of 30 km/h; the recommended 30 km/h speed limit moves in the right direction but a posted limit on its own typically does not bring 85th-percentile speeds to the posted level (council’s own baseline data records 85th-percentile speeds above the posted limit at multiple Wellington Street sites). The Primary mechanism the auditors specified, physical speed cushions, is not delivered.9

The consultation report shows majority support for change, but officers have recommended something more limited. Across all respondents in council’s own consultation, 64% support a change option in Clifton Hill and 62% in Collingwood.3 On the locals-only cut officers cite as their basis for declining the change options, support and opposition are at parity in both sections. What’s been recommended is neither of the two change options that were consulted on. Detail in the section on local support below.

The opposition’s concerns are refuted on the agenda’s own evidence

Two claims have driven most of the opposition: that the proposal will close Wellington Street, and that it will flood local side streets with traffic. The staff report contradicts both, and also sets out additional mitigations if any side-street impact does occur.

“11,000 cars will be displaced.” The 11,000 figure on the local signs is roughly double the actual two-way traffic on the Clifton Hill section of Wellington Street, which is about 5,600 vehicles a day in council’s own measurements.10 Still bad in Clifton Hill, but Wellington Street in Collingwood carries the additional burden of traffic going between the Eastern Freeway and the CBD.

“Side streets will be flooded.” Staff write directly that “the results of preliminary traffic modelling do not support these concerns and officers have identified additional measures to manage any traffic diversion that does occur.”11 Council’s modelling for Gold Street in Collingwood (the worst-case street in the analysis) shows a potential increase of 900 vehicles per day from a base of 1,600, which staff describe as keeping Gold Street “within the range for a local road.”12 Staff also note the modelling is “likely being an overestimation of traffic impacts,” and that “the modelling work does not show that large volumes of traffic will use other local roads nearby.”13

Mitigations are available if any side-street increase does occur. In response to community feedback, council commissioned an additional external study identifying mitigation measures specifically for Gold Street and the east-west streets that connect to it, such as Noone Street.14 These include physical traffic-calming measures and a partial traffic barrier at the Gold and Sackville Streets roundabout, which the consultants say “significantly reduces the appeal of Gold Street as a north-south through route.”15 These measures were not on the table during the consultation that closed in October 2025; they appear in the agenda for the first time.

For fuller detail on side-street traffic, see the Traffic page.

“Road closure.” Staff are explicit that the proposed traffic barriers are not a road closure: “It is important to clarify that Council has never proposed the complete ‘closure’ of Wellington Street to traffic. A modal filter is not a road closure. Modal filters are a traffic management measure that prevents through car traffic whilst allowing local traffic movement for local access. People walking and cycling are able to bypass the blocks. Traffic management and modal filters specifically are commonly used across Yarra and Australia.”16 The proposal stops cars cutting through; it does not block local access. People can still drive to and from any home in the area. There are at least 40 such barriers already in place across Yarra, including Page Street next to Clifton Hill Primary.

Local support for change is already there

The consultation showed support for change over doing nothing, even while the concerns about closures and side-street flooding remained uncorrected. Headline support across all respondents is 64% in Clifton Hill and 62% in Collingwood. On the locals-only cut staff use as the basis for declining the change options, the result is 53/47 in favour of change in Clifton Hill and 50/50 in Collingwood.3

Doorknocking in the streets most affected by the proposal has found support higher than the consultation figures suggest, once residents have had a chance to talk through what is and is not being proposed. The agenda now corrects the headline misunderstandings on closures and side-street traffic on the public record.
